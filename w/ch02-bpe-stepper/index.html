What to notice

Frequent strings collapse first and the gains flatten quickly: the first few merges (␣t, ␣th, ␣the) do most of the work on English text. Pre-tokenization decides what can ever become one token (with the digit rule, 1234567 is always at least 3 tokens), and a script the corpus never saw stays at raw bytes, 2 to 3 tokens per character.
Model (same algorithm as Exercise 2.3.1). Training: start from the 256 single-byte tokens, split the corpus into pre-token chunks, and repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair across all chunks (ties go to the lowest pair IDs). Encoding: inside each chunk, apply the earliest-learned merge that is present until none applies, which is what tiktoken does. The small bundled corpus runs out of pairs after about 130 merges, while production tokenizers learn 100,000 to 200,000.
