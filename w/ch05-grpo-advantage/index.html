Group relative policy optimization (GRPO): advantages inside one group

Try this

  1. Defaults: G = 8 answers per prompt, 3 correct. Read the advantages with "divide by std" (standard deviation) on (GRPO), then off (Dr. GRPO). Then move the correct count from 1 to 7.
  2. Drag the pass rate p toward 1 for G = 8, then for G = 16, and find where half of all groups give zero advantage.
  3. Set the lengths to 200 tokens (correct) and 2,000 tokens (wrong) and flip the aggregation between sequence-mean (GRPO) and token-mean (DAPO, decoupled clip and dynamic sampling policy optimization).

What to notice

The group is its own baseline: no value model. Dividing by the std inflates the advantage of a rare success or a rare failure. When every answer is right (or every one wrong), all advantages are 0 and the prompt teaches nothing; bigger groups delay that. Sequence-mean averaging divides each answer's push by its own length, so a long wrong answer is pushed down no harder than a short one; token-mean fixes that.
Model (as in 5.5). Rewards ri ∈ {0, 1}. Advantage Ai = (ri − mean(r)) / std(r) with std on (population std), Ai = ri − mean(r) with std off; an all-equal group gives A = 0. Sequence-mean token weight = 1/(G·|oi|), where |oi| is answer i's length in tokens; token-mean weight = 1/Σj|oj|. Zero-advantage share for a prompt with pass rate p: pG + (1 − p)G.
