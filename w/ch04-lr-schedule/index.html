What to notice

WSD spends far longer at the peak LR (about 1.6× the area under the curve of cosine here) and can produce a finished checkpoint from any point on its plateau by running a short decay branch. Cosine has to know the run length in advance, because its whole shape stretches with the total number of steps.
Model (as in 4.3). Both schedules warm up linearly from 0 to the peak LR over the warmup steps. Cosine then decays as floor + (1 − floor)·½(1 + cos(π·progress)), times the peak. WSD holds the peak until the final decay share of the run, then decays linearly to 0. A WSD branch at step b decays linearly to 0 over (decay share × b) extra steps. Area is the mean LR over the run, divided by cosine's.
