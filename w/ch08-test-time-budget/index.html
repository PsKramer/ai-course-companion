What to notice

Parallel samples are almost free in wall-clock time while they fit in one wave, because decode is memory-bound: every step reads the 16 GB of weights once whatever the batch size. Once the KV cache fills, extra samples need another wave and the time steps up. When p is tiny, no affordable n helps: at p = 0.01 you need 230 samples for 90% coverage.
Model (same as Exercise 8.1.1). Coverage assumes independent samples and a perfect verifier. Decode time for a batch of B sequences: Σi<length (2N + B·(prompt + i)·131,072 bytes) / 3.35 TB/s. That is the memory-bound decode model from 1.3: weights (2 bytes × N parameters) plus each sequence's KV cache, read once per step from high-bandwidth memory (HBM). A wave holds ⌊56 GB / ((prompt + length) × 131,072 bytes)⌋ sequences, and waves run one after another. Cost = GPU-seconds × price / 3,600. Prefill time is ignored because it is under 1% here.
