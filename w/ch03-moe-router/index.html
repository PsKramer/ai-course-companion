Mixture-of-experts (MoE) router: who gets the tokens?

Try this

  1. Defaults: Course-MoE (48 layers, 128 routed experts plus 1 shared expert, top-8 routing) with a 512-token batch and balancing off. Press "Step" a few times and read max/mean load and idle experts.
  2. Switch balancing to "bias (auxiliary-loss-free)" and press "Run 50 steps". Watch the smoothed max/mean fall; tap through a few tokens and see that their gates still come from the affinities alone.
  3. Move top-k to 2 and to 16 and read active parameters and FLOPs per token.

What to notice

Without balancing, a few experts take several times the mean load while others sit idle. The bias shifts which experts get selected until loads even out, but gates are still computed from the affinities alone, so a token's mixing weights are not distorted by the bias. Active parameters, and so FLOPs per token, grow with k; total parameters do not change.
Model (as in 3.5). Each token is a 64-number feature vector drawn around one of 6 cluster centres; a fixed random router matrix (with a few experts favoured a priori, to create imbalance) gives sigmoid affinities s. Selection uses s + bias; gates use s only, normalized over the k chosen experts (Step 5). Bias update after each batch: bi ← bi − γ·sign(loadi − mean). Auxiliary loss: one gradient step per batch on α·E·Σ fiPi applied to the router matrix. Parameter counts are Course-MoE's (Step 3): per layer attention 37.75 million, router 4096·E, each expert 3 × 4096 × 1536 = 18.87 million; active adds the unembedding (525 million), not the input lookup. A single 512-token batch has only 32 slots per expert at top-8, so its max/mean never gets much below about 1.5 from sampling noise alone; the smoothed value shows the trend.
