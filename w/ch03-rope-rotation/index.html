Rotary position embedding (RoPE): the clocks inside attention

Try this

  1. Set the base θ to 10,000 and drag the query position m from 0 to 200 (key position n stays 0). Watch the dials turn and the score curve oscillate and sag.
  2. Switch θ to 500,000 (Course-8B): the slow dials barely move, and the score curve decays more slowly.
  3. Set the context to 32,768 tokens (training length 8,192) and push m past 8,192: the slow dials turn red. Then turn on position interpolation.

What to notice

The score oscillates and its envelope sags as distance grows, because the fast pairs fall out of phase; with a larger base θ more pairs stay in phase, so it sags more slowly. Pairs whose wavelength is longer than the training length only ever turn part of the way in training; past that length their angles are new to the model. Interpolation squeezes positions back into the trained range, at the price of finer spacing for the fast pairs.
Model (as in 3.3). Head dimension dh = 128, so 64 pairs; pair i turns by ωi = θ−2i/128 radians per position; wavelength 2π/ωi positions. The score uses one fixed random unit vector (seeded) as both query and key, so it is Σi (size of pair i)² · cos((m − n)·ωi), the part of q·k that RoPE controls. With interpolation, m and n are divided by s before rotating.
