All-reduce: how long does summing a buffer across GPUs take?

Try this

  1. Defaults: 8 GPUs on NVLink 4 (450 GB/s per GPU, 5 µs per step), 16 GB (Course-8B's bf16 gradients), ring and tree. Drag the message size from 1 KB to 16 GB and find where ring overtakes tree.
  2. Set 512 GPUs and the InfiniBand NDR preset (50 GB/s, 10 µs) and repeat.
  3. Turn on hierarchical (8 GPUs per node) at 512 GPUs and read the time for 16 GB; then go back to 8 GPUs on NVLink and turn on NVLink SHARP (in-switch reduction).

Ring–tree crossover S* = nβα(n − 1 − log2n)
Bytes each GPU sends (ring), 2(n − 1)/n · S

What to notice

Small messages and many GPUs are latency-bound: the ring needs 2(n − 1) steps in a row, so trees, with 2·log2n steps, win. Large messages are bandwidth-bound and the ring wins, because each GPU sends only 2(n − 1)/n · S instead of 2S. Hierarchy keeps most bytes on fast NVLink inside a node, and in-switch reduction sends each byte once.
Model (alpha-beta model, as in 7.5). α = fixed latency per step, β = bandwidth per GPU. Ring: 2(n − 1)α + 2(n − 1)/n · S/β. Tree: 2·log2(n)·α + 2S/β. NVLink SHARP (Scalable Hierarchical Aggregation and Reduction Protocol: the NVLink switch does the sum; NVLink presets, n ≤ 72 only): 2α + S/β. Hierarchical: ring inside each node on NVLink 4 (reduce-scatter and all-gather, 2[(g − 1)α + (g − 1)/g · S/β]), plus a ring of n/g nodes over the selected link on the S/g shard each GPU owns. Bus bandwidth = 2(n − 1)/n · S / T. Latencies 5 µs and 10 µs are assumptions.
