Compute budget: how big a model for my FLOPs?

Try this

  1. Defaults: Course-8B's budget, C = 7.2 × 10²³ FLOPs (8.03 billion parameters × 15 trillion tokens × 6). Slide model size N from 3 billion to 300 billion parameters and watch predicted loss.
  2. Switch the fitted constants from the Epoch 2024 refit to Hoffmann et al. method 3 and watch the optimum move from about 81 billion to 36 billion parameters.
  3. Switch to lifetime mode and raise the expected served tokens S to 50 trillion.

What to notice

The valley is flat near the optimum: halving or doubling N costs only about 0.005 in loss (nats per token). The two published fits disagree a lot about where the optimum sits. Once you count serving, the expected number of served tokens pushes the best model far below the Chinchilla size, which is why Course-8B is trained on 15 trillion tokens.
Model (as in 4.2). Predicted loss L(N, D) = E + A/Nα + B/Dβ in nats per token, where N = parameters, D = training tokens, and E, A, B, α, β are fitted constants. Fixed compute: D = C / (6N) (the 6ND rule from 1.2), and the optimum minimizes L over N. Lifetime mode: the target loss is Course-8B's (8.03 billion parameters, 15 trillion tokens) under the chosen fit; for each N the tokens needed are D = (B / (L* − E − A/Nα))1/β (no value where the target is unreachable), and lifetime compute = 6ND training + 2N·S inference. H100-hours assume 40% model FLOPs utilization (MFU) of 989 TFLOP/s.
