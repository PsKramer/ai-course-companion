Attention heatmap: who looks at whom

Try this

  1. Keep the sentence "The cat sat on the mat because it was tired" and tap the token "it". Look at layer 1, then layer 5 (head 3): the weight moves onto "cat".
  2. Turn the causal mask off and on: with it off, early tokens put weight on later tokens, which the model never saw in training.
  3. Turn on "grouped-query attention (GQA) probe" and "group view": four heads share one set of keys but still attend differently, because their queries differ.

What to notice

Early layers spread weight over nearby tokens; by the middle layers some heads lock onto the word "it" refers to ("cat", 0.84 in layer 5, head 3), and in the repeated-phrase sentence a layer-9 head jumps to the token that followed the earlier copy. Each row is a softmax, so its weights sum to 1. Without the mask the rows change, because the scores for future tokens were never trained. Heads that share keys still differ, because each has its own query.
Data. Real attention computed on a CPU with NumPy from GPT-2 small (124 million parameters, 12 layers, 12 heads per layer, head dimension 64; OpenAI, MIT license), not from Course-8B. Each cell is softmax(q·k / √64) over the row; scores for future tokens were computed without the mask so the "mask off" view can be shown. GPT-2 uses multi-head attention (one key-value head per query head). The GQA probe mean-pools the keys of each group of 4 heads (the conversion recipe of Ainslie et al., 2023, without the extra training that recipe adds) and scores them against each head's own query; it shows the shape of the idea, not a trained GQA model.
