The roofline: memory-bound or compute-bound?

Try this

  1. Defaults: H100 in bf16, Course-8B's feed-forward up+gate GEMM (general matrix multiply, 4096 × 28,672) at M = 1 token. Slide M to 8, 64, 512 and 8,192 (prefill) and watch the dot climb the slope and reach the flat roof.
  2. Switch the chip to B200 or TPU7x, then the precision to FP8 (8-bit floating point): does any point change side?
  3. Turn on the shared-memory roof and step the tile size up until the tile dot reaches the shared-memory ridge, on H100 and on B200.

What to notice

Batch size moves a GEMM along the slope: at M = 1 it does about 1 FLOP per byte read and uses 0.3% of an H100. Changing precision moves the roof and the dot together (half the bytes, double the peak), so nothing changes side; only the times shrink. Attention over the KV cache stays at h/hkv FLOP per byte whatever the batch. On B200 the HBM ridge barely moves but the shared-memory ridge doubles, so kernels need bigger tiles.
Model (as in 7.1). Attainable FLOP/s = min(peak, I × bandwidth), with I the arithmetic intensity in FLOP per byte. GEMM M × K × N: F = 2MKN FLOPs, Q = b(MK + KN + MN) bytes, b = bytes per number. Decode attention: I = 2·(h/hkv)/b. RMSNorm (root-mean-square normalization): about 1 FLOP/byte. Shared-memory roof: SMs (streaming multiprocessors) × 128 bytes × clock; a square tile of side B does B/2 FLOP per byte of shared memory in bf16 (BMBN/(BM + BN) in general). Roofline time = max(F/peak, Q/bandwidth). Chip figures are dense datasheet peaks.
